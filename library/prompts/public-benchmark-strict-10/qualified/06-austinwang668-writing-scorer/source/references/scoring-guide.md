## Rating method

Rate these four criteria separately:

1. Task Achievement for Task 1 or Task Response for Task 2
2. Coherence and Cohesion
3. Lexical Resource
4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

Treat the integer bands as performance anchors. Award the highest band whose positive characteristics are consistently supported by the response, then check for a limiting feature. Do not assemble a higher score from isolated strengths. Use a half band only when performance is sustained above the lower anchor but does not fully meet the upper anchor.

Keep the criteria independent. For example, inaccurate chart data primarily constrains Task Achievement; a relevant but grammatically weak argument may still earn a higher Task Response score than Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

## Shared criterion anchors

The task-specific criterion is defined in [task-checklists.md](task-checklists.md). Use the following anchors for the other three criteria.

### Band 9

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Make the message effortless to follow; manage paragraphing and links so naturally that they almost never draw attention.
- **Lexical Resource:** Use vocabulary with exceptional range, precision, appropriacy, and flexibility; allow only extremely rare slips.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Control a wide structural range and punctuation throughout; allow only extremely rare slips with negligible effect.

### Band 8

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Sequence ideas logically, manage cohesion well, and use paragraphs appropriately; allow only occasional lapses.
- **Lexical Resource:** Express precise meanings flexibly with a wide range; allow occasional, low-impact collocation, word-choice, spelling, or formation errors.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Use a wide range flexibly; make most sentences error-free and keep occasional errors non-systematic and low-impact.

### Band 7

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Maintain logical organisation and clear progression, with flexible referencing and linking despite a few inaccuracies or imbalances.
- **Lexical Resource:** Show enough range for flexibility and precision, including some less common language; make only a few clarity-neutral errors.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Use varied complex structures with generally good control and frequent error-free sentences; let remaining errors not impede meaning.

### Band 6

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Maintain a coherent overall progression, but allow some mechanical, faulty, missing, or overused links and less flexible referencing or paragraphing.
- **Lexical Resource:** Use generally adequate task-appropriate vocabulary, though range or precision is restricted and some non-impeding errors occur.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Mix simple and complex forms, but show limited flexibility and weaker accuracy in complex sentences; errors rarely block communication.

### Band 5

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Show visible organisation but incomplete logic or progression; connect sentences unevenly and use cohesion or paragraphing inadequately.
- **Lexical Resource:** Use a limited but minimally sufficient range with repetition, simplification, and noticeable errors that sometimes burden the reader.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Rely on a limited, repetitive structural range; attempt faulty complex forms and make frequent errors that sometimes obstruct reading.

### Band 4

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Present ideas without coherent ordering or clear progression; rely on basic, repetitive, inaccurate, or missing links and references.
- **Lexical Resource:** Use basic, repetitive, sometimes task-inappropriate language; let word-choice, formation, or spelling errors impede meaning.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Use very few structures, mostly simple; make frequent grammar and punctuation errors that may impede meaning.

### Band 3

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Show no apparent logical organisation; make relationships difficult to identify and use very few meaningful cohesive signals.
- **Lexical Resource:** Provide an inadequate resource, often dominated by errors or overdependence on input or memorised strings; meaning is severely compromised.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Attempt sentence forms, but let grammar and punctuation errors dominate and prevent most meaning from coming through.

### Band 2

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Show little evidence of control over organisation.
- **Lexical Resource:** Produce only a few recognisable strings with essentially no control of word formation or spelling.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Show little or no evidence of sentence forms beyond possible memorised fragments.

### Band 1

- **Coherence and Cohesion:** Fail to communicate a meaningful message.
- **Lexical Resource:** Show no usable resource beyond isolated words.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Show no rateable sentence control.

### Band 0

Use only for no attempt, an answer wholly in a language other than English, or proven total memorisation. Never infer proof of memorisation from generic phrasing alone.

## Boundary calibration checks

Run these cross-checks before finalising a boundary score:

- **Band 6 versus 7 for Coherence and Cohesion:** Decide from sustained logical progression across the whole response. A long paragraph or a few conspicuous linking words do not by themselves cap a response at Band 6 when paragraph purposes and idea sequencing remain clear.
- **Band 6 versus 7 for Lexical Resource:** Scan the whole response, not only the correction examples. Award Band 7 when the range permits flexibility and precision and the comparatively few errors do not reduce clarity; do not infer a high error rate from a table deliberately showing the most useful corrections.
- **Band 6 versus 7 for Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Sample sentences throughout every paragraph. Frequent error-free sentences and successfully controlled complex structures support Band 7 even when several local or recurring errors remain, provided they do not impede communication.
- **Band 7 versus 8:** Require wide, flexible, well-managed performance with only occasional lapses. Do not award Band 8 for a polished introduction or a few sophisticated phrases when control elsewhere remains uneven.
- **Error impact:** Separate visibility from prevalence. A memorable spelling or collocation error can be easy to quote but still rare in a long response; a simple systematic error may deserve more weight even when each occurrence looks minor.

Treat the correction table as illustrative feedback, never as an error census. Complete the band decision from the entire unedited script before selecting examples for the table.

## Length and special cases

- Expect at least 150 words for Task 1 and 250 words for Task 2.
- Do not apply an invented flat deduction below the minimum. Let the missing development and the reduced evidence of language control affect the applicable descriptors.
- Apply Band 1 to a response of 20 words or fewer as specified in the public descriptors.
- Discount text copied from the question or rubric when judging the candidate's own achievement and language resource.
- Treat bullet points, notes, or fragments instead of connected prose as a format and development problem. Do not automatically set every criterion to the same band.
- Do not use Band 0 for an off-topic but genuine attempt; use the appropriate low-band task anchor instead.

## Score calculation

Average the four criterion bands equally for a task. Round the result to the nearest half band with these boundaries:

- fractional part below `.25`: round down to the whole band
- `.25` through below `.75`: round to `.5`
- `.75` or above: round to the next whole band

Calculate a complete Writing estimate as:

`(Task 1 band + 2 × Task 2 band) / 3`

Then apply the same half-band rounding. Task 2 therefore contributes two thirds of the estimate. Use `scripts/band_score.py` rather than mental arithmetic.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Quote only short excerpts that occur exactly in the response.
- Link each reason to observable performance and the relevant band boundary.
- Distinguish a systematic pattern from a one-off slip.
- Give fewer correction examples rather than inventing or paraphrasing an “original” error.
- Use ±0.5 as a normal estimate range. Widen the range for unclear handwriting, incomplete transcription, or a genuinely borderline script.
- Omit Task Achievement/Response and the overall task band when the prompt or source material needed to judge relevance and accuracy is missing.
