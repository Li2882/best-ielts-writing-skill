## Scoring model

Each task is judged on four equally weighted criteria:

1. **Task Achievement (TA)** for Task 1 or **Task Response (TR)** for Task 2
2. **Coherence and Cohesion (CC)**
3. **Lexical Resource (LR)**
4. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA)**

Task 2 contributes twice the weight of Task 1 to the Writing result. Public descriptors use whole-band criterion judgements. Practice estimates may average those judgements and report the nearest half band.

## Criterion meaning

### Task Achievement — Academic Task 1

Check whether the response:

- uses an appropriate connected-text format;
- selects and highlights the main features rather than mechanically listing every detail;
- provides a clear overview of major trends, stages or differences;
- groups information and makes relevant comparisons;
- supports the overview with accurate, relevant data or details;
- avoids unsupported explanations or opinions.

### Task Achievement — General Training Task 1

Check whether the letter:

- makes its purpose clear;
- covers every bullet point;
- develops each point with relevant detail;
- maintains an audience-appropriate tone;
- uses a suitable letter format without unnecessary addresses.

### Task Response — Task 2

Check whether the essay:

- addresses every instruction and part of the question;
- presents a clear, relevant position throughout;
- develops focused main ideas rather than merely listing them;
- explains and supports claims with relevant reasoning or examples;
- avoids irrelevant, repetitive or over-general material.

### Coherence and Cohesion

Check overall progression, logical sequencing, paragraph focus, relationships between ideas, reference/substitution, and accurate—not mechanical—use of cohesive devices.

### Lexical Resource

Check range within the task, precision, appropriacy, collocation, register, flexibility, spelling and word formation. Uncommon vocabulary helps only when natural and accurate.

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy

Check meaningful variety of sentence structures, control of simple and complex forms, sentence boundaries, agreement, tense, articles, clauses and punctuation. Judge how often errors occur and how much they impede communication.

## Task-specific band ceilings

Apply only when clearly supported by the response:

- Missing or unclear Academic Task 1 overview normally prevents a strong TA result; select the best-fitting official anchor rather than using an automatic numeric deduction.
- Omitting a GT Task 1 bullet point, using an unclear purpose, or sustaining an unsuitable tone limits TA according to severity.
- Failing to answer part of a Task 2 prompt, lacking a discernible position, or leaving main ideas undeveloped limits TR.
- Lists, notes or bullet-only responses are inappropriate formats for the Writing tasks.
- Short responses provide less evidence and often cannot fulfil the task; do not invent a universal fixed penalty beyond the public 20-word rule.
- Repeated formulaic or memorised-looking language does not demonstrate flexible LR or GRA control.

## Evidence and feedback policy

- Quote only short spans from the candidate response.
- Tie each score to observable language or task fulfilment.
- Separate errors that impede meaning from minor slips.
- Explain the next-band gap in concrete terms.
- Present the result as a diagnostic estimate, never as an official IELTS score or examiner certification.
